## Why Swete (and not Rahlfs / CCAT)

Rahlfs–Hanhart (Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft) is in copyright, and the CCAT/CATSS
morphological LXX that most free tools derive from is distributed under a user
declaration that restricts redistribution. We could not verify that either may
be republished on a public website, so they are not used. Swete's edition is in
the public domain, and the Open Greek and Latin transcription is openly licensed
(CC BY-SA 4.0), which permits public redistribution with attribution and
share-alike. Consequence: the Greek OT/Apocrypha data in `docs/data` and
`corpora/lxx` is a derivative of a CC BY-SA work and must stay under CC BY-SA 4.0
with attribution to OGL/First1KGreek when published.

Alternatives considered: `nathans/lxx-swete` (a token-per-line derivative of the
same OGL files; lacks Ecclesiastes too and drops structure we need, so we parse
the TEI directly), `eliranwong/LXX-Swete-1930` (no explicit license on the
digitization — not used).

### Known limits of the Swete transcription

* It is machine-corrected OCR. Expect stray letters and occasional misread
  words (e.g. `Νπββουχοδονοσὸρ`). Mixed Latin/Greek look-alike capitals are
  mapped back to Greek; stray digits are removed.
* **Ecclesiastes is missing** from First1KGreek (`tlg0527.tlg030` has no text).
* Some verse numbers are defective. The loader repairs a number that sits
  between two consecutive neighbours (e.g. `220` between 19 and 21 → 20); every
  repair is logged in `build/alignment_report.json` (`swete_verse_number_repairs`).
  Cases the rule cannot decide are listed in `sources/swete_fixes.json`
  (Judith 9:19→14; Wisdom chapters labelled 16–20 are 15–19; Psalm 91 LXX has
  vv. 15–16 merged, so its last verse is declared as 16 for the TVTMS tests).
* Genuine Septuagint gaps (e.g. Codex B's shorter 1 Samuel 17–18) are kept as
  gaps, not repaired.
* For Daniel, Susanna and Bel the table uses Theodotion's version; Swete's Old
  Greek versions are in `corpora/lxx/all_lines.json` (ids `DanOG.*` etc.).
* The Odes (Swete follows Codex A's order, with an unnumbered split of Ode 4)
  are omitted from this prototype.

## Versification method

1. Each text is loaded with its **own** numbering (Luther 1912 and the WLC follow
   Hebrew numbering; the Vulgate and Swete follow Latin/Greek; SBLGNT follows NA).
2. For every native verse, the build looks up the TVTMS expanded-data lines with
   that source reference and evaluates each line's tests against the text's own
   verse inventory (`=Exist`, `=NotExist`, `=Last`, `TextBeforeV1`, and
   word-count comparisons such as `Mal.3:23*2<Mal.3:22+Mal.3:24`). The first line
   whose tests all pass gives the standard (KJV/KJVA) reference. No passing line
   → the reference is already standard. Code: `scripts/polyglot/tvtms.py`.
3. Refinements, all documented in code:
   * word counts are letter-run tokens (so Hebrew maqaf-joined words count);
   * a `Concatenation` line only applies if its own `!0`/`!a` sub-line also
     passes (TVTMS sometimes puts the discriminating length test only there);
   * `IfEmpty` lines are ignored (our verses always have text);
   * `CopiedFrom` (LXX duplicates such as 3 Kgdms 2:35a–o, 12:24a–z) stays at its
     own position as an extra row instead of doubling the parallel verse;
   * unnumbered psalm titles in Swete follow wherever that psalm's verse 1 maps;
   * Swete's Greek Esther additions are lettered in Swete's own scheme, which
     TVTMS does not describe; the loader maps them to KJV-Apocrypha Esther
     10:4–16:24 (`_esther_fixed` in `scripts/polyglot/sources.py`).
4. **Local changes to TVTMS** (TVTMS asks for a note of changes):
   `sources/tvtms_corrections.json`. Currently one: the Septuagint order of
   Malachi 4:4–6 (Elijah / turn the hearts / law of Moses) was listed as the
   inverse permutation; corrected for both the `Greek` (Swete, ch. 4) and
   `Greek2` (Rahlfs-style 3:22–24) lines. Worth reporting upstream.
5. Where one native verse covers several standard verses, the text sits in the
   first row and the following rows show "↑ text shown above"; where several
   native verses fall into one row (e.g. Hebrew Ps 51:1–2 = the title), they are
   joined and labelled `51:1–2`.

### Independent check

`scripts/qa_alignment.py` learns a crude word-association dictionary from the
aligned rows themselves and flags rows whose neighbour (±1–2) matches better than
the row itself. Flag rates after the build: Hebrew–English 0.64 %, Greek–English
1.1 %, Latin–English 0.72 %, German–English 0.26 %, Greek–Latin 0.89 % — mostly
repetitive lists (Numbers 33). Remaining clusters worth a look: Vulgate 1 Macc 1
(Latin has 67 verses vs Greek/KJVA 64; TVTMS has no Latin rule), LXX 3 Kgdms 6
(the Septuagint rearranges the temple chapter), and the Vulgate's Tobit and
Judith, which are Jerome's own recensions and align by number only.

## Record shape of the new corpora

`corpora/vulgate/all_lines.json` (35,809 verses) and `corpora/lxx/all_lines.json`
(29,120 verses incl. the Old Greek Daniel/Susanna/Bel) use the same fields as
the other instances (`play_id`, `canonical_id`, `location`, `act`, `scene`,
`line_num`, `speaker`, `text`) with the **native** reference as
`canonical_id`, plus `native_ref` (as printed, e.g. `3 Kgdms 2:35a`) and
`standard_id` (the KJV-spine row used here).
